# Saath user manual

Saath (साथ, "together") helps a person with a long-term illness such as diabetes, and the people who look after them, share care. It works without internet for the person's own safety and routine. Everyone has their own phone and their own account.

This manual has one chapter per kind of user, then the shared rules. Find your chapter, follow the steps.

## 1. Who is who

There are five kinds of people. Which one you are is decided when you start.

| Who | What they are for | Their phone shows |
|---|---|---|
| **The person who needs care** (the patient) | Has the illness, or is getting older. Their phone watches for falls, reminds them, and tells their circle if something is wrong. | Today, Circle, Safety, Care, Plan, and Me |
| **Guardian** | A family member who looks after the person (for a teenager, a parent). | One screen: "People I look after" |
| **Neighbour** | Someone close by who can help quickly. | The same one screen |
| **ASHA worker** | A community health worker who visits households. | The same one screen, plus visit reminders |
| **Doctor** | Plans the patient's care and sees their numbers. | One screen: "My patients" |

Two things to know:

1. **The person who needs care decides the role of everyone else.** A guardian, neighbour or ASHA worker does not pick their own role. The patient chooses it when adding them, and it applies only to that patient. The same person can be a guardian to one patient and a neighbour to another.
2. **A doctor is a separate kind of phone.** A doctor is only ever a doctor. They do not choose "guardian" or "neighbour" and cannot be one.

A person can also be both: someone who needs care and also looks after someone (choose "Both" in **Me**).

## 2. Getting started

### Install

Copy `saath.apk` to your Android phone and open it. Allow "install from this source" if asked. Saath needs Android 8 or newer and works on low-end phones.

### The first screen

Saath asks **How will you use Saath?** Pick one:

| Choice | For |
|---|---|
| **I need care** | The patient |
| **I look after someone** | A guardian, neighbour or ASHA worker who was invited |
| **I am a doctor** | A doctor |

At the top you can switch between **English** and **हिन्दी**. You can change this any time in **Me**.

### If you chose "I need care"

Four short steps, with a progress bar.

1. **Welcome**: choose the language, and who will use the phone: **Teen**, **Elder** or **Adult**. This changes the Today screen (see chapter 3).
2. **About you**: your name, and optionally your condition. Saath says plainly that it never reads your messages, contacts or location, and movement data stays on the phone.
3. **Who should we reach?** Add the people Saath should call if you have a fall and do not answer. For each one: name, phone number, and **who they are** (Guardian, Neighbour, ASHA or Doctor). Add at least one real number. You can remove someone with their Remove button.
4. **How should alerts work?** Five switches, all off to start. Each asks the phone for permission when you turn it on: call my contacts automatically, send the alert SMS by itself, loud alarm when no one answers, remind me at these times, keep watching in the background. You can change all of them later in **Me**.

Tap **Start using Saath**.

### If you chose "I look after someone"

1. **About you**: your name.
2. **Your account**: create an account with **the same phone number the person gave when they added you**. This matters: the invite only works for that number.
3. **Join**: enter the code the person sent you, or scan their QR. You can skip this and join later from your one screen with **I have an invite code**.

You do not choose a role. It comes from the invite.

### If you chose "I am a doctor"

1. **About you, doctor**: your name as patients will see it.
2. **Your account**: create an account with your phone number.
3. **Join**: enter a patient's code or scan their QR, or show your own card so a patient can add you (see chapter 7).

### Accounts

An account is a phone number and a password. There is no text message, so it costs nothing.

- You can use Saath without an account. Everything stays on this phone, and it is lost if you clear Saath's data or change phone. Saath warns you about this.
- To make or use an account, open **Me**, tap **Sign in or create account**, enter your number and password, and tap **Continue**. If the number is new it creates the account; if it exists it signs you in.
- A forgotten password cannot be reset. Write it down.
- Guardians, neighbours, ASHA workers and doctors need an account, because it is how Saath knows which circle they belong to.

## 3. The person who needs care

You have six places. The bar floating at the bottom has five: **Today**, **Circle**, **Safety**, **Care**, **Plan**. **Me** is the round avatar with your initial at the top right.

### Today

- **Greeting card**: today's date and how many of your tasks are done.
- **I'm OK**: tap once to tell your circle you are fine. It also counts as activity, so the phone does not think you have gone quiet.
- **Today's slips**: a stack of small cards for today. See "Suggestion cards" below.
- **Today's tasks**: your routine, such as morning medicine, lunch, evening walk, sugar check. Tap the circle to tick one. Edit them in **Me**.
- **Medicines now**: the medicines due now, if you have added any in **Care**.
- **A plan notice**: if your doctor published a care plan, a card says who set it. Tap **Got it** to dismiss.

**Teen, Elder and Adult** screens show the same things arranged differently. A **Teen** also has the stage ladder (see Plan) and the school plan. An **Adult** sees small statistics and a trend line. An **Elder** gets bigger, simpler cards.

### Suggestion cards

The stack shows up to seven small ideas for today.

- **Care** cards (pink tag) are made on your phone from what you entered: an open task, a refill running low, test strips, a foot check, the weekly check-in. They always come first and never need internet.
- **Idea** cards (yellow tag) are written by an AI from a short anonymous summary of your day. They need internet. They are things like a walk, water, a stretch, or a call to someone. They never include doses or diagnoses.

For each card:

- **Stamp it**: you did it. A stamp lands and the card tears off.
- **Open**: jumps to the place the card is about (when there is one).
- **Not today**: puts it aside. Saath learns which small things you actually do and shows fewer of the ones you skip.

In a hard week or a crisis phase (set in **Me**), you only get gentle cards about rest and company.

### The helper

The round sparkle button floats above the bar. Tap it for a chat window.

- Ask about **your health and daily life**: food, water, walking, sleep, mood, your routine, or how to use Saath. Four quick questions are offered.
- It will **politely refuse anything else** (money, news, maths, shopping). It only says it can help with your health, food, routine and Saath.
- It **never gives a dose** of any medicine and never says what a sugar number means. For medicine questions it tells you to follow your prescription and ask your doctor or pharmacist.
- If you type something that sounds like an emergency (a fall, chest pain, very low sugar), **nothing is sent anywhere**. Saath shows a message and an **Open Safety** button, and tells you to press the alert or call someone or 112.
- The window shows a notice that an AI writes the replies. The trash icon clears the chat. The chat stays on your phone.
- It needs internet. Without it, it says so.
- You can turn the helper and the Idea cards off in **Me**, under **Smart cards and helper**.

Only the person who needs care gets the helper and the Idea cards. Guardians, neighbours, ASHA workers and doctors do not.

### Safety

This is the watching part. At the top it says whether Saath is **Watching** or **Watching in background**.

**What it watches for**

- **A fall**: a sharp impact followed by the phone staying still for a few seconds.
- **Long quiet**: no movement for much longer than usual (the default is 6 hours), outside your sleep hours (about 10 pm to 7 am).

Saath cannot promise to catch every fall, and it does not sense blood sugar.

**When it thinks something is wrong**

1. The screen shows **Are you OK?** with a 30 second countdown and a loud tone.
2. Tap **I'm OK** to cancel. Tap **False alarm** if the phone was wrong, so it learns.
3. If you do not answer, it moves through your contacts one by one, about 20 seconds each. It shows WhatsApp, SMS and Call buttons for each, and can do some of this by itself if you turned on the automatic switches in **Me**.
4. A loud alarm plays here if you turned that on and no one is reached.
5. Everyone in your circle gets the safety alert, whatever their privacy setting.

An automatic call counts as "someone answered" if it lasts about 50 seconds. Android does not say whether a call was answered, so that is a stated guess.

**How Saath learned from you**: a short plain-words list of your personal thresholds, with **Reset** to start again.

**Heart-rate band** (optional): connect a cheap Bluetooth band. Saath shows the live rate and a calm note if it stays high or low while you are still. It is a note, not an alarm. It needs a real band.

**Sensor trial mode**: tap **Missed fall** or **Missed quiet** if Saath should have alerted and did not; it adjusts. It also shows counts, and **Export trial CSV** shares them. Because real contacts could be reached during a test, it warns you if the automatic call or SMS switches are on.

### Care

Sugar, medicine and costs.

- **My medicines**: add a medicine with its **name**, the **dose exactly as written on your prescription**, and **when** you take it (for example after breakfast). Saath records what you typed; it never suggests, calculates or changes a dose. Tick them off in Today.
- **Sugar log**: tap **Add sugar reading** and type the number (mg/dL). Choose **7, 14 or 30 days** to see the average, lowest, highest and number of readings, a line chart, and a daily-average bar chart. Saath shows numbers, never "good" or "bad".
- **Medicines taken this week**: a bar chart of how many you took each day.
- **Heart-rate** statistics for this session, if a band is connected.
- **Medicine stock**: how many pills you have left and when you run out, so refills are not a surprise.
- **Care budget**: add what you spend on medicine, strips and travel, and see the trend.

If you logged insulin and have not eaten for a while, Saath shows a reminder card with plain safe steps (such as a sugary food). It never says how much insulin to take.

### Plan

Handover, visits and healthy habits. Tap a row to open it.

| Section | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Handing over care** (teens) | A ladder of stages as a teenager takes more responsibility. A parent can be asked to agree before each step. |
| **Visit prep** | A one-page summary of recent facts for a clinic visit, and a place to add your questions. |
| **Health passport** | A card with your name, condition and, only if you choose, medicines, sugar and an emergency number. Shows as a **QR code** or a **PDF** you can share. It works offline. |
| **Weekly check-in** | One tap on a face for how the week felt. A bad week makes Saath gentler. |
| **Food guide** | Meal ideas made on the phone for your region and diet (veg or not), using common cheap foods. Tell it what you liked or want less of. Marked "verify locally". |
| **Body check** | A foot check list, and a diabetes risk questionnaire that needs a waist measurement. It is screening, not a diagnosis. |
| **School plan** (teens) | A card for school staff. Sharing it is your own tap and is logged. |

### Circle

Your circle is the people who look after you.

- **Safety alerts always reach everyone and cannot be turned off.** The rest is your choice.
- **Privacy dial**: each person has a switch, **In my privacy dial**. Off means they only get safety alerts. On means they also see more, and you choose **Routine** or **Full**. See chapter 8.
- **Add a person**: tap the button, then type their **name** and **phone number**, and choose who they are: **Guardian**, **Neighbour**, **ASHA** or **Doctor**. Tap **Add and send code**. This does two things at once: the person becomes someone Saath calls in an alert, and they get an invite. A share sheet opens so you can send the message by WhatsApp or SMS. A **QR code** and a **code** are shown to scan or type.
  - If you choose **Doctor**, the form tells you a doctor sees your sugar readings and charts, and you can change that any time.
- **Manage** (on each person): **Time off** (pause routine updates from you for a while; safety alerts still go), **Show my number** (lets the circle see your number), and **Remove**.
- **Ask someone to do a task**: ask for help with one of your tasks for a set time.
- **Who viewed**: a log of who looked at what, so you can see.
- **I have an invite code**: if you are the one joining someone else's circle.

Each person appears once. Adding them again with the same name and number does not duplicate them.

### Me

- Your name and a badge for who the phone is for.
- **Keep watching in the background**, **Call my contacts automatically**, **Send the alert SMS by itself**, **Loud alarm when no one answers**.
- **How I use Saath**: I need care, I look after someone, or Both.
- **Account**: sign in or create an account, or skip.
- **Shared phone** (see chapter 9).
- **Who is this for** (Teen, Elder, Adult) and **Where are you now** (New diagnosis, Stable, Hard time).
- **Settings**: language, **Look** (Phone, Light or Dark), **Large text**, **Smart cards and helper**.
- **Edit routine**: opens a bottom drawer to rename, retime, add or remove tasks.
- **Reset app data**: wipes everything on this phone.

## 4. Guardian

A guardian is a family member the patient chose. For a teenager, a guardian also gives consent.

**Your screen**: **People I look after**, with one row per person and a number at the top. Anyone with an open alert is shown first and the top card turns red.

**Tap a person** to open their panel:

- Your role with them ("You are their guardian") and what you can see.
- Any **open alert**, shown clearly, with how long ago.
- A **feed** of what they have shared, as far as their privacy dial allows.
- **Pause routine updates from them** (the Time off switch): stops routine news but never safety alerts.

**What you see** depends on the dial they set for you. A guardian is kept at **Routine** at least, and for a teenager Saath will not let that go lower. At Routine you see tasks done and "I'm OK" check-ins. At Full you also see summaries.

**Getting an alert**: a banner appears on any screen when someone you look after may have fallen, until you say you have seen it. If the app is closed and push notifications are set up, it arrives as a notification. Call the person or someone near them.

**To join a circle**: choose **I look after someone**, create your account with the number the patient gave, and enter their code or scan their QR.

**For a teenager**: a guardian is asked to agree in the **Handing over care** ladder. A teen needs a guardian's consent to use Saath. Saath records a guardian's name and number. It does not verify identity and is not a legal compliance claim.

## 5. Neighbour

A neighbour is there to help fast in an emergency. The role is deliberately light.

- You get **safety alerts**: falls and long quiet. That is the default and the minimum.
- You see nothing about routine, medicines, sugar or the person's health unless the patient raises your level, which is rare for a neighbour.
- If you get an alert, call the person or go and check, and call someone from their circle if you cannot reach them.
- You can **pause routine updates** from them, but safety alerts still reach you, always.

**Your screen**: the same **People I look after** list, one row per person. Tap a person to see alerts and your role ("You are their neighbour").

To join: choose **I look after someone**, create your account with the number they gave, and enter the code or scan the QR.

## 6. ASHA worker

An ASHA worker visits households. The role adds a visit reminder.

**Your screen**: the same **People I look after** list. Next to each household:

- **Visit due** (amber) appears when it has been **14 days** since your last visit, or you have never visited.
- Tap a household to open it.

**In a household**:

- A **Household visit** card shows how long ago your last visit was (or "never").
- Tap **I visited today** to record it. The reminder resets for 14 days.
- Your visit log stays **on your own phone** and is not sent to anyone.
- You see what the patient's privacy dial allows. By default that is safety alerts; the patient can raise it.

**What an ASHA worker does not do here**: Saath does not diagnose, prescribe or calculate doses. It tells you the facts the patient has shared and when a household is overdue.

To join: choose **I look after someone**, create your account with the number the patient gave, and enter the code or scan the QR.

## 7. Doctor

A doctor has their own kind of phone. You are only ever a doctor, never a guardian or neighbour.

**Your screen**: **My patients**. A number at the top says how many. A patient with an open alert is shown first and the card turns red.

### Getting patients

Two ways. Either way, nobody types details twice.

- **The patient adds you**: the patient taps **Add a person**, chooses **Doctor**, and sends you the code or QR. You choose **I am a doctor**, create your account with the number they gave, and enter the code or scan the QR.
- **You show your card**: on your screen tap **Add a patient**. It shows a QR card with your name and number (you must be signed in). The patient scans it with their camera, Saath opens **Add a person** already filled in as a Doctor, they confirm once, and they show you a code. Scan or type it to join.

Invites are for one phone number, work once, expire after 14 days, and lock after ten wrong tries. A forwarded code is useless to anyone else.

A doctor sees **only circles where they were invited as a doctor**. If someone invited you as something else, Saath shows a small note that those were set aside.

### In a patient's panel

Tap a patient.

- **Sugar readings**: all their logged readings, with the last reading, average, lowest, highest, count, a line chart and a daily-average chart, for 7, 14 or 30 days. It is **read-only** and shows numbers without saying what they mean; reading them is your job. You only see readings while the patient has you at **Full** in their privacy dial. If none appear, it says none have been shared yet.
- **Care plan for this person**: tap **Edit care plan**. In a drawer you can add **medicines** (name, dose exactly as on the prescription, time of day), **tasks** with an hour, how many **sugar checks per day**, and a **note** for the patient. Tap **Publish plan**. The patient's phone applies the newest plan by itself the next time it is online and open, and shows your name on it. Saath records what you type and does not suggest or calculate doses.
- **Alerts and feed**: any safety alert, and shared updates, as far as the patient's dial allows.
- **Pause routine updates** from them, if you wish. Safety alerts always still reach you.

A doctor does not get the helper button or the suggestion cards.

## 8. Privacy: who sees what

The patient controls this with the **privacy dial**, one per person in their circle.

| Level | Name | What that person receives |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Safety only** | Falls and long-quiet alerts. Nothing else. This is where everyone starts, except doctors (see below). |
| 1 | **Routine** | Plus tasks done and "I'm OK" check-ins. |
| 2 | **Full** | Plus summaries. For a doctor, also the sugar readings. |

Rules that never change:

- **Safety alerts always reach everyone in the circle.** The dial cannot turn them off.
- A person can **pause routine updates** from someone, but safety alerts still arrive.
- A phone number is shown to the circle only if its owner chose **Show my number**.
- The patient can see **who viewed what**.
- Data on the phone is not encrypted, and raw movement data never leaves the phone.

**Doctors start at Full.** The patient names a doctor in the invite, and the form says a doctor sees readings and charts. That is how the doctor gets the numbers. The patient can lower it any time in **Circle**. The patient's phone sends readings to the server only while a doctor at Full is in their circle. If the patient lowers it, the doctor stops seeing them. Because health records may be involved, this needs legal review (India's DPDP Act).

**What the AI is sent**: a short anonymous summary of the day (language, time of day, who the phone is for, how the routine is going, a few yes/no facts). Never a name, phone number, medicine, dose, sugar number, contact or location. Chat messages you type are sent to be answered, except anything that sounds like an emergency.

## 9. Settings, language, dark mode, shared phone

**Language**: **Me > Settings > Language**. English or हिन्दी. It changes at once.

**Look**: **Me > Settings > Look**: **Phone** (follows your phone), **Light** or **Dark**. Close Saath and open it again to apply.

**Large text**: **Me > Settings > Large text**.

**Edit routine**: **Me > Edit routine**.

**Smart cards and helper**: **Me > Settings**, for the person who needs care only. Switch the AI cards and the helper off or on.

**Shared phone**: for a phone several people use. In **Me**, turn on **Shared phone** and set a 4-digit PIN. Each person has their own data and PIN, and a lock screen asks who you are. A wrong PIN says how many tries are left. The PIN keeps casual eyes out; it is not strong protection.

**Reminders**: turn on **Remind me at these times** to get a reminder at each task's hour, even when Saath is closed.

**Permissions**: the phone asks the first time you turn on calling, SMS, notifications or Bluetooth. If you refuse, the switch stays off and says why. You can change it in Android settings.

## 10. If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Sign in or create account" says the server is not set up | The build has no server. Everything else works offline. |
| An invite code does not work | It must be used with an account made with the **same phone number** the person typed. It works once, expires after 14 days, and locks after ten wrong tries. Ask for a new one. |
| A guardian or doctor sees nothing | Check what the patient's dial for them is (chapter 8). A doctor sees readings only at **Full**. |
| The helper says it needs internet | You are offline, or the service is busy. Try again later. Your Care cards still work. |
| The helper refuses a question | It only answers about your health, food, routine and Saath. |
| No Idea cards, only Care cards | You are offline, the helper is switched off in Me, or it is a hard week. |
| The automatic call or SMS switch will not stay on | Saath needs the phone's permission. Allow it in Android settings. |
| Dark mode did not change | Close Saath fully and open it again. |
| A fall alert went off by mistake | Tap **False alarm**. Saath learns from it. |
| I forgot my password | It cannot be reset. Make a new account, or keep using Saath without one. |
| I want to wipe everything | **Me > Reset app data**. This cannot be undone. |

**In an emergency, do not use the helper.** Press the alert on the **Safety** tab, call someone in your circle, or call **112**.

## 11. What Saath does not do, and what has been tested

**Saath never**:

- calculates, suggests or changes a dose of any medicine or insulin,
- diagnoses, or says what a sugar or blood-pressure number means,
- promises to catch every fall,
- senses blood sugar,
- reads your other apps, messages or contacts,
- sends anything to a call or SMS service that costs money.

**What has been run, and what has not.** This is the honest status at the time of writing.

| Area | Status |
|---|---|
| Alert countdown, privacy dial rules, fall and quiet detection logic, care rules | Built and unit-tested. A real fall test worked on a phone. |
| Setup, Today, Circle, Care, Plan, Safety, Me, drawers, dark mode, Hindi | Seen on an Android emulator. |
| Suggestion cards, helper (including refusals) | Run against the live service. Only a handful of real replies have been read. |
| Doctor sees a patient's readings, doctor care plans, joining by invite or QR, ASHA visit reminders | Built and tested against a fake server. **Not yet run with two real phones.** |
| Push notifications to a helper's phone, Bluetooth heart-rate band | Built. **Not run on real devices.** |
| Hindi wording | Complete and checked for parity. **No native-speaker review yet.** |
| Food and suggestion wording | **Needs a clinician or dietitian review before real use.** |
| Accessibility | Automated checks only. TalkBack has not been tried. |
| iPhone | Configured, never built or run. |

If you are a clinician or Hindi speaker asked to review, the wording is listed in `docs/REVIEW_SHEET.md`. Technical detail is in `docs/` (see `README.md`).
